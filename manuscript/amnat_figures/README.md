# SCH ecological Major Article — SVG figure package v1

## Status and scientific ownership

These five editable vector SVGs are figure **candidates for the parallel American Naturalist Major Article**, not evidence of journal submission or a change of the SCH programme's primary New Phytologist Viewpoint route.

- figure_01_theory.svg — SCHEMATIC THEORY, *not* fitted flowers or empirical compromise loss.
- figure_02_identification.svg — SCHEMATIC 2×2×2 experimental design across architecture, pollinator and antagonist settings, *not* an executed SCH/Pedicularis factorial.
- figure_03_state_optima.svg — SCHEMATIC five-level z comparison across four proposed ecological states. The curves and peak ranks are invented solely to explain the design; never analyze them as source measurements.
- figure_04_ecological_routes.svg — **REAL PUBLISHED SOURCE FIGURE**, containing four independent published studies, distinct ecological outcomes, and qualitative/quantitative findings reproduced only on their original scales.
- figure_05_handoff.svg — SCHEMATIC conditional SCH→SLK/BITA inference; no observed L, Phi, architecture cost K or trait splitting.

Each figure is self-contained SVG with editable vectors and retained SVG title and description, not a raster screenshot. The five-figure count plus one paper Table 1 uses all six planned combined display slots. A journal portal may require print-quality PDF/EPS/TIFF exports. Vector SVGs are **not yet evidence of author/editorial-approved print layout**.

## Draft captions

**Figure 1. Shared trait use, conflict and theoretical mismatch.** A multifunctional coordinate z can influence two functions without those functions favoring different phenotypes. The curves in B and C are illustrative only. If the pure function optima theta_1 and theta_2 differ and the two component functions are approximated quadratically with fixed weights, their idealized mismatch has cost L = a b/(a+b) (theta_1−theta_2)^2. This theory identity does not describe a field-estimated SCH loss or measured historical modularization.

**Figure 2. Experimentally identified versus pure functional components.** A proposed 2×2×2 factorial crosses shared/differentiated architecture A with pollinator P and antagonist G settings (eight cells). Trait-conditioned fitness contrasts across selective states can identify local consumer contributions, but only verified randomization and common reproductive outcomes allow causal interpretation. Quantitative component optima cannot be promoted to pure function-specific preferences without prospective context-stability/equivalence checks. All treatments and causal links are schematic and have not jointly been carried out in the focal Pedicularis system.

**Figure 3. Distinct ecological-state and pure-function trait optima.** Four hypothetical reproductive surfaces W00, W10, W01 and W11 across five floral-trait ranks illustrate that state-specific optimum shifts can be examined experimentally. Neither those curves nor the displayed peaks come from original field data. The optimum observed with only one ecological consumer state is not automatically the pure functional optimum of that consumer, because background costs and interactions may persist.

**Figure 4. Four ecological locations where selection can be reassembled.** (A) *Gymnadenia conopsea*: selection on flowering onset under open versus supplemented pollination, with natural versus excluded herbivory (original selection beta ± SE, Sletvold et al. 2015, Appendix A, Table A2, doi:10.1890/14-0119.1). The herbivore-excluded pair (+0.094 versus −0.066) has individually supported opposite directions; these plotted whiskers are **source SE**, not multiple-testing-adjusted confidence intervals. (B) *Lythrum salicaria*: simulated apical clipping increases mean inflorescence number and delays flowering while weakening selection for additional inflorescences and strengthening selection for earlier flowering (Thomsen & Sargent 2017, Tables 1–2, doi:10.1093/aob/mcx026). Group mean dates do not identify individual flowering durations, and eight originally assigned control stems were reassigned after natural damage. (C) *Brassica rapa*: positive nominal visitor and oviposition associations of corolla size and phenylacetaldehyde alongside positive net seed-fitness selection on corolla size across three insect-composition regimes (Knauer & Schiestl 2017, Tables 1–3, doi:10.1007/s10682-016-9878-8). The receiver coefficients arise from **distinct response models** and must not be pooled. (D) *Pedicularis rex*: reported six-site puncture-plus-drainage site-by-treatment tests are significant for predation but not detected for initial or final seed production (Sun & Huang 2015, Table 2, doi:10.1093/aobpla/plv019). These different significance decisions do not test equality of effects across endpoints. Insect treatments/observed flower traits cannot be interpreted as a shared randomized trait-by-consumer experiment. Each quadrant refers to a separate biological study, not an independently reproduced common mechanism.

**Figure 5. Downstream theoretical handoff conditional on SCH identification.** Only an empirically validated, common-fitness-scale conflict loss L can be transported into an SLK architecture-value analysis or tested through additional BITA trait dimensions. In the theory, recoverable loss times the recoverable fraction s must exceed the added architecture cost K for Phi = sL−K to be positive. Neither this diagram nor available source studies estimate the full theoretical terms, characterize historical trait splitting or establish a fitness-improving differentiated architecture. The figure is entirely schematic.

## What Figure 4 does NOT plot

The 14-edge/24-edge exploratory pollination selection-spectrum analysis is retained in manuscript §§7.8–7.9 and corresponding detailed source ledgers, **not overplotted into Figure 4**. Those families have different multiplicity and rounding-sensitivity ceilings and are neither more independent studies nor pooled coefficients. Figure 4 instead makes the paper's final cross-system ecological proposition visually legible: receiver preference, induced plant phenotype, population pressure, and viable reproductive performance can change at different stages.

## Visual/export verification status

Structural and numerical source checks are reproducible in tests/test_sch_amnat_vector_figures.py. The source SVGs are committed, editable and use font-family fallback Arial/Helvetica/sans-serif without embedded or distributed font binaries. Programmatic XML checks cannot prove actual typographic fit, grey-scale legibility, minimum publisher print size or exported PDF fidelity. **Manual rendered visual review and journal-accepted export QA remain pending** until publication author approval.
