# Three-chapter contract v1 — SCH

## Chapter order

```text
Chapter 1 / SCH
where does a shared trait compromise settle?

Chapter 2 / BALANCE
why does the compromise regime persist, and what is its internal geometry?

Chapter 3 / BITA
when and how does differentiated architecture become favourable?
```

## SCH responsibility

SCH owns the **shared-coordinate world**. Its primary outputs are:

- state-specific optima such as `z_P*`, `z_G*`, `z_C*` where experimentally identified;
- pure-function optima only when independently identified;
- a one-axis conflict/compromise surface;
- a fitness-scale compromise budget where the component reconstruction is identified.

SCH does **not** own persistence inside the interval between conflict onset and architecture switching, and does not claim historical trait differentiation.

## Handoff to Chapter 2

BALANCE receives, where available:

```text
L(e) = fitness-scale compromise/conflict load
```

plus the environmental control axis and uncertainty/identification limits.

Chapter 2 then combines that receipt with decoupling and architecture-cost information to study

```text
Phi(e)=s(e)L(e)-K(e)
```

inside the region `L>0, Phi<0`.

## Handoff onward to Chapter 3

The Chapter-2 critical surface `Phi=0` defines the context at which BITA's differentiated architecture becomes competitive. BITA then tests dimensional release and mechanism identification rather than re-estimating the SCH compromise as its primary estimand.

## Claim ceiling

The three chapters are nested research questions, not evidence that a historical sequence SCH -> BALANCE -> BITA occurred in any focal lineage. Historical transition requires independent phylogenetic or temporal evidence.
