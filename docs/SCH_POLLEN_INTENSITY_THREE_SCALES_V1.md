# SCH pollen limitation and selection: three-scale original-source audit (2026-11-15)

## Supported experimental biological result (2016)

Sletvold & Ågren (2016), *Ecology* 97:3091–3098, DOI 10.1002/ecy.1554 (original published article/abstract https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecy.1554) **experimentally changed pollinator access** for five different exposure durations in one *Gymnadenia conopsea* population. Pollen limitation ranged from 0 to 0.77 and opportunity for selection (variance in relative fitness) increased approximately five-fold. Pollinator-mediated selection was stronger in the most pollen-limited treatment versus open pollination: **plant height +91%**, **corolla size +34%**, **spur length +330%**. Original study also reported that the trait–fitness covariance for number of flowers declined as PL increased, while other focal trait covariances changed less.

This is a single independent original **experimental programme**, not five independent populations or three independent studies. Its original plant-level data and source-estimate covariance were NOT ingested here, and none of the published percentages should be treated as an inverse-variance-compatible pooled beta.

The specific experimental result means that reducing pollination access can amplify selection associations for multiple flower-related traits, even though selection on other traits is partly buffered by covariance changes. It is experimental manipulation of interaction intensity, **not randomized assignment of a floral phenotype**, not direct functional optima, and not SCH loss L.

## Positive published across-species result (2017)

Trunschke, Sletvold & Ågren (2017), *New Phytologist* 214:1381–1389, DOI 10.1111/nph.14479, studied 12 species (one population each, five correlated trait axes). Original Table 1 across species and two pollination regimes:

| Response: opportunity for selection | Original source F(1,20) | P |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pollen limitation | **16.78** | <0.001 |
| Pollen supplementation | **14.34** | 0.001 |
| PL × pollination treatment | **15.60** | <0.001 |

This original 12-species × two-treatment opportunity comparison is **not** the 60 species-trait rows of the separate original Table 4 selection model. The original Table 4 results remain as previously audited: PL versus absolute pollinator-mediated selection F(1,50)=**18.79**, P<0.001; net F=**6.374**, P=**0.015**; nonpollinator-mediated F=**0.0045**, P=**0.947**. The absence of significance for nonpollinator selection is not a proof of equivalence to zero. PL is a calculated population-level measure, not itself an independently randomized cross-species treatment. Species phylogeny, independent species sampling and correlated source estimates remain unaddressed.

Thus this is source-backed among-species **trait-selection intensity association and selection-opportunity moderation**, not a newly demonstrated general causal law.

## Separate within-species nonassociation (2014)

Sletvold & Ågren (2014), *Evolution* 68:1907–1918, DOI 10.1111/evo.12405, studied 14 population-year units in two orchid species (*Gymnadenia conopsea* 9, *Dactylorhiza lapponica* 5). Within each species, pollen limitation did not detectably explain temporal/spatial variation in pollinator-mediated trait selection. The 2016 same-population **interventional** PL variation and 2017 among-species **observational** PL variation are different targets, not contradictory tests of a universal fixed slope.

| Study | Ecological scale | Change / conditioning | Directly supported ecological statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | Same species across population years | Naturally fluctuating PL | No detected within-species PL predictor of trait-selection components |
| 2016 | One population, five randomized pollination-intensity regimes | Manipulated pollinator access | Higher experimental PL accompanies greater opportunity and stronger selection on three floral traits |
| 2017 | One local population per 12 orchid species | Naturally varying mean PL, supplemented vs natural arms | Between-species PL linked to pollinator-mediated and net selection, as well as a PL × pollination effect on opportunity |

The mechanistic implication is narrower than “PL predicts selection everywhere”: realized phenotypic selection depends on both the **mean fitness constraint imposed by interaction intensity** and the **trait-dependent mapping to pollination/fecundity**, which can vary across ecological contexts. A positive 2016 experimental response alongside an unsupported 2014 within-species correlation is compatible with interaction opportunity being real while local trait–fitness covariance or competing gradients vary. This exact cross-scale mechanism remains **hypothesis generating**, not tested as a single interaction/meta-effect by the present source tables.

## Identification and dependency boundaries

- One paper ≠ 12 independent experimental studies; five intensity levels ≠ five independent populations.
- One source's original ANCOVA F/P results cannot certify a full 12-species cluster-robust comparison unless relevant raw estimates and covariance are available.
- No independent 2017 Dryad raw refit: its XLS has pinned metadata but public 401/403 access in the current Actions environment, as recorded in `docs/SCH_ORCHID_2017_RAW_ORIGINAL_ACCESS_BOUNDARY_20261115.md`.
- No original 2016 raw plant rows, no formal within-vs-between ecological-scale coefficient contrast, no new same-coordinate G/P causal optima and no SCH L.
- No new studies are added to the original 137 paired beta denominator, 2017 original table audits, prospective holdout or NPH Viewpoint.
