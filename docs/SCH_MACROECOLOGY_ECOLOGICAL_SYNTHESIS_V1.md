## Result 1 — multifunctional geometry is heterogeneous

Across all 48 canonical axes:

The primary static fixed-role subset contains 19 resolved canonical axes from 13 independent biological clusters: conflict 9, reinforcement 2, one-sided/null 8.

These are descriptive counts, not natural prevalence.

The ecological conclusion is qualitative but strong:

> The presence of a pollinator–antagonist setting does not determine a single trade-off geometry.

## Result 2 — geometry belongs to a trait axis, not to a species or paper

The clearest within-system example is Gymnadenia conopsea.

~~~text
flowering phenology:
pollinators -> later flowering
herbivores  -> earlier flowering
=> conflict + cancellation

spur length:
pollinators -> longer spur
herbivores  -> longer spur
=> reinforcement
~~~

Different trait coordinates inside the same biological system can therefore resolve into different functional geometries.

## Result 3 — weak net selection can hide strong ecological conflict

The Gymnadenia phenology axis contains opposing pollinator- and herbivore-mediated selection with similar additive strength.

~~~text
strong positive component
+
strong negative component
≈ weak net selection
~~~

Weak observed net selection therefore does not necessarily mean weak ecological selection.

Treatment-cell beta ± SE values are now recovered from Appendix A Table A2. Cancellation magnitude based on mediated agent contrasts remains fail-closed because contrast uncertainty/covariance is not reported in Table A2.

## Result 4 — context changes several different parts of the geometry

H2 no longer uses one undifferentiated “context dependent” label.

Current descriptive change types include:

- geometry-class switch — Gentiana lutea colour;
- geometry disappearance — Caryopteris divaricata corolla tube;
- component-weight shift — Pedicularis rex exsertion;
- component-weight shift with evolutionary response — Primula farinosa scape-height morph;
- consumer-role behavior shift — blueberry and sesame.

This is biologically stronger than a generic statement that effect sizes vary among environments.

## Result 5 — consumer identity is not a fixed functional sign

The canonical evidence contains ROLE_DEPENDENT and BENEFIT_COST_COUPLED consumers.

Examples include nectar robbers that also pollinate, the same bee species switching legitimate and robbing behavior, pollen-feeding beetles damaging floral structures while pollinating, and brood pollinators coupling pollination with later seed consumption.

Thus a taxonomic or behavioral label such as robber, florivore or seed parasite is not enough to assign a fixed ecological sign.

## Result 6 — source-level relevance is not the same as identified geometry

The source audit actively demotes biologically relevant studies when the required same-axis geometry is absent.

Examples include Pulsatilla, the Costa Rican patch-context hummingbird study, Haplopappus, the Vaccinium congener comparison and Camissoniopsis.

The empirical identification result is:

> The literature frequently contains the relevant ecological ingredients without containing the estimand required for a functional-conflict claim.

## Current ecological synthesis

The strongest current biological statement is:

> Multifunctionality produces a family of ecological geometries rather than a universal compromise. Realized geometry is assembled by the focal trait axis, the local interaction regime and the functional role expressed by interacting consumers.

A compact representation is:

~~~text
realized geometry
=
g(
  trait axis,
  consumer role,
  ecological context
)
~~~

Possible outcomes now recovered include opposition, reinforcement, one-sided/null response, cancellation, context-variable geometry, geometry disappearance, component-weight shift, consumer-role switching and benefit-cost coupling.
