# SCH Darwin-finches cross-domain audit v1

## Question

Can a non-floral system support SCH's broad claim that one shared morphological coordinate can be pulled by competing functional demands?

## System

Darwin's finches, especially `Geospiza fortis` and comparative Galapagos finch datasets.

Candidate shared morphology:

```text
beak / jaw morphology and associated jaw mechanics
```

Functional lanes:

```text
feeding / seed processing
vs
rapid jaw movements used in vocal performance.
```

## Primary-source evidence recovered

### Feeding performance

Herrel et al. 2005, Functional Ecology, DOI:

```text
10.1111/j.0269-8463.2005.00923.x
```

measured bite force directly in 147 `G. fortis`. Beak depth, width and shape predicted bite force; the paper links deeper beaks to the ecology of cracking harder seeds.

### Vocal / signal performance

Podos 2001, Nature, and Huber & Podos 2006, Biological Journal of the Linnean Society, show that larger/deeper/wider beaks covary with lower-frequency / reduced vocal-performance song features within and among Darwin's finches.

### Direct biomechanical trade-off

Herrel et al. 2009, Functional Ecology, DOI:

```text
10.1111/j.1365-2435.2008.01494.x
```

measured bite force and jaw movement during song and found a negative force-velocity relation within and among species, largely independent of body size and phylogeny.

## SCH interpretation

This is a strong **cross-domain shared-constraint candidate**:

```text
higher force capacity / feeding mechanics
<->
slower jaw movement / constrained vocal mechanics.
```

It demonstrates that a multifunctional morphological apparatus can face an opposed performance relation outside floral ecology.

## What is not identified

The existing evidence does not provide the registered SCH causal surface:

```text
z x function-1 weight x function-2 weight
```

and does not directly estimate:

```text
z_F1*
z_F2*
z_C*
fitness-scale conflict budget L
```

on a common reproductive-fitness scale.

The force-velocity relation is a performance trade-off; it is not yet a causal optimum-shift experiment.

## Programme role

```text
G2_CROSS_DOMAIN_OBSERVATIONAL_SHARED_CONSTRAINT_CANDIDATE
```

Use this system to show that the *kind* of one-apparatus multifunctional constraint represented by SCH is not floral-specific. Do not count it as a causal replication of the SCH mechanism.

## Next gate for stronger promotion

A stronger SCH-style use would require a design that links a manipulable shared mechanical coordinate to two function-specific fitness/performance surfaces and demonstrates opposed optimum shifts or independently measured function optima.

## Claim ceiling

Appropriate:

> Darwin's finch jaw/beak mechanics provide a non-floral example in which force-oriented feeding performance and rapid movement used in vocal performance are constrained by the same morphological apparatus.

Not appropriate:

> Darwin's finches causally prove the SCH shared-optimum mechanism or estimate the SCH conflict budget.
