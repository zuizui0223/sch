# SCH evolutionary-outcome primary-source audit v1

## Decision summary

The primary literature supports a positive but bounded hypothesis-recovery statement.

1. **An integrated one-trait compromise is empirically recoverable.** Pérez-Barrales et al. found that pollinator selection for larger *Dalechampia* bracts was counteracted by seed-predator selection and that net selection tended to be stabilizing. Theis and Adler independently showed the negative side of the same logic experimentally: enhanced fragrance increased florivore rather than pollinator attraction and reduced seed production. These studies support the existence of a one-trait balance; they do not establish its prevalence.
2. **Maintenance can also occur as a context-dependent polymorphism rather than one intermediate optimum.** In *Primula farinosa*, pollinators and antagonists generated frequency-dependent selection whose sign varied among years and populations. Longer-term manipulations and observations then linked spatially varying pollinator-versus-grazer selection to rapid changes in the frequencies of a genetically based floral-display dimorphism.
3. **Cue separation is recovered at the mechanism level, but not yet as a complete evolutionary outcome.** Component-specific Petunia scent perturbations show that different compounds in one bouquet can have host-location and deterrent functions. *Biscutella* conditionally induces a cue shared by bees and crab spiders after florivore attack, providing state-dependent gating rather than a private cue. *Nicotiana* scent and nectar can be experimentally uncoupled, but both axes still increase mutualist service and antagonist exposure. No audited study demonstrates that dual-audience selection caused a cue system to evolve from shared to private while preserving pollination benefit on a common plant-fitness scale.
4. **Population differentiation is supported; lineage branching is not.** *Primula* field manipulations and multiyear morph-frequency changes provide causal microevolutionary evidence. *Biscutella* common-garden contrasts provide evidence consistent with population differentiation in inducible scent. A multigeneration *Brassica* selection experiment shows that herbivory changes pollinator-driven evolutionary trajectories. None of these reconstructs a historical split from one integrated signal into pollinator-maximizing and antagonist-avoiding lineages.

Accordingly, the strongest current SCH claim is:

> Shared or jointly exposed floral traits can generate opposing selection. Real systems show at least three bounded responses: a stabilizing net-fitness compromise, context-dependent maintenance of alternative display morphs, and population-level divergence when the balance of mutualists and antagonists changes. Component partitioning and conditional emission show plausible routes to partial decoupling, but the evolution of private cues and lineage branching remain untested in the audited evidence.

This audit does **not** alter the frozen four-field coverage gate or its counts. In particular, it does not promote any source to a strict one-trait experimental pass.

## Question and adjudication rules

The focal one-trait quantity is the net consequence of an attraction/display axis,

\[
S_A(A)=M_A(A)-G_A(A),
\]

where `M_A` is pollination benefit and `G_A` is antagonist cost. This audit asks what happens evolutionarily after mutualists and antagonists act on `A`:

- **integrated compromise:** one phenotype is retained near an internal net-fitness optimum, or an integrated display remains under opposing selection;
- **within-individual modularization/separability:** components, timing or states within the display differentially regulate the two receiver channels;
- **polymorphism:** alternative values of the same display persist within populations;
- **population differentiation:** heritable display differences track different mutualist-antagonist regimes;
- **lineage branching/specialization:** descendant lineages evolve toward distinct audience-specific optima from an ancestral integrated state.

Evidence is called **direct** only for an outcome actually measured by the focal source. Manipulating a cue and measuring insect response is direct mechanism evidence but only indirect evidence for maintenance or divergence. A comparative association is direct evidence for the observed phenotype pattern but indirect evidence for its selective cause. Historical branching requires more than extant population differences: it needs replicated lineage transitions, an explicit ancestral or transition analysis, and evidence connecting those transitions to the audience regime.

## Outcome-by-outcome recovery

### 1. Integrated compromise: recovered, with two distinct maintenance forms

The cleanest continuous-trait result is Pérez-Barrales et al.: the same bract-size axis increased both pollination opportunity and seed-predator exposure, and their combined fitness function tended toward stabilizing selection. This directly matches the prediction that, when `M_A` and `G_A` rise together but with different shapes, their difference can have an internal optimum.

The *Primula* work identifies a second form of maintenance. A single intermediate phenotype is not required if the relative fitness of alternative display morphs depends on their frequency and ecological context. Toräng et al. show that rare-morph advantage can occur but that its sign varies; Ågren et al. show that spatially varying mutualist-antagonist balance changes morph frequencies. The defensible statement is therefore not “trade-offs always maintain one intermediate trait,” but “trade-offs can maintain integrated variation either through an internal optimum or through context-dependent balancing processes.”

Theis and Adler provide a complementary boundary: increasing fragrance moved the system past a beneficial balance, attracting florivores without a detected pollinator gain and reducing seed production. This is direct evidence for the negative side of the net-fitness curve, but it alone does not locate the optimum.

### 2. Within-individual cue modularization: mechanism recovered, evolutionary origin unresolved

Two non-equivalent mechanisms are visible.

- **Component partitioning:** Kessler et al. 2013 show different volatile constituents within one Petunia bouquet with host-location versus deterrent functions. This is the closest direct evidence that a complex display can contain separable functional modules. The missing test is a common factorial experiment showing that the deterrent component reduces `G_A` while the pollinator-attractive component preserves `M_A` and increases common plant fitness.
- **Conditional gating of a shared cue:** Knauer et al. show increased β-ocimene emission after florivore attack, especially in populations where the crab-spider receiver can become beneficial by removing florivores. This is a timing/state solution: the plant retains a shared cue but deploys it when the antagonist's sign changes. It is not a private signal.

Kessler et al. 2015 demonstrate that floral scent and nectar can be independently manipulated, but both increased hawkmoth oviposition as well as pollination-related outcomes. Independent biosynthetic control is therefore not sufficient evidence of ecological separability.

### 3. Population divergence: recovered; specialization remains bounded

Ågren et al. provide the strongest causal bridge from selection to population change: interaction manipulations changed relative fitness, the selection regime predicted subsequent morph-frequency change, and long-term grazer removal altered that frequency. Knauer et al. add replicated common-garden population differences in inducible β-ocimene associated with the presence versus absence of crab spiders. Ramos and Schiestl show in experimental evolution that adding herbivory redirects the trajectory caused by bee selection.

Together these sources license a positive claim that mutualist-antagonist balance can alter population evolutionary trajectories. They do **not** show that populations optimize opposite ends of a shared cue axis in a universal way. In *Primula*, the comparison is long versus short display under pollination and grazing; in *Biscutella*, the differentiated phenotype is inducibility of a cue whose antagonist can become a mutualistic natural enemy; in *Brassica*, the antagonist is a leaf herbivore and the evolved response includes mating-system change.

### 4. Lineage branching: not recovered

No audited source establishes all of the following:

1. an ancestral integrated/shared cue;
2. replicated descendant shifts toward pollinator-maximizing versus antagonist-avoiding phenotypes;
3. a measured or experimentally supported change in the balance of the two receiver channels;
4. heritable divergence extending to reproductive isolation or independent lineages.

Page et al. use interspecific recombinant hybrids and identify cue effects on a seed-eating pollinator's host choice, but they do not estimate the same cue's pollination benefit or reconstruct why the two *Silene* lineages diverged. Theis et al. compare cucurbit taxa, but do not model trait transitions or common fitness. These are useful candidate-system locators, not branching tests.

| Shared cues couple mutualist and antagonist responses | `RECOVERED_MECHANISM` | Shared attraction recurs in an assembled evidence base and is directly demonstrated for β-ocimene in one field system | Predeclared prevalence sampling and comparable within-study response coefficients |
| Coupling can flatten or reverse net fitness | `RECOVERED_CASE_LEVEL` | Direct field studies show stabilizing net selection in one system and a negative fitness consequence of signal enhancement in another | Comparable effect surfaces across systems; uncertainty-bearing common-outcome estimates |

| Cue components or timing can reduce the dilemma | `PARTIAL_MECHANISM` | Component partitioning and state-dependent emission are demonstrated | A common experiment showing preserved `M_A`, reduced `G_A`, and improved net plant fitness after decoupling |
| Different mutualist-antagonist balances cause population divergence | `RECOVERED_BOUNDED` | Field manipulation and multigeneration experiments show redirected evolutionary trajectories and microevolutionary change | Replication specifically on floral shared cues and explicit cue-overlap measurements |
| The trade-off causes lineage branching/specialization | `NOT_TESTED` | No affirmative historical claim | Replicated comparative transitions, ancestral-state model, audience-regime data, and tests against abiotic/phylogenetic alternatives |

## Consequence for the next audit

The frozen existence screen remains:

\[
A\text{ manipulated} + \text{pollinator response} + \text{antagonist response} + \text{common fitness outcome}.
\]

Evolutionary outcomes should be coded in a separate second stage, not inserted into that gate. The additional fields should distinguish:

1. `selection_form`: directional, stabilizing, disruptive, frequency-dependent, context-dependent, or not measured;
2. `cue_architecture`: single shared coordinate, component-partitioned bouquet, conditionally expressed shared cue, private/separable cue, or unknown;
3. `evolutionary_level`: same-generation fitness, within-population polymorphism, population-frequency change, population differentiation, lineage divergence, or not evaluated;
4. `causal_strength`: cue manipulation, consumer-context manipulation, multigeneration experimental evolution, common-garden comparison, observational selection, comparative association, or synthesis;
5. `claim_ceiling`: the strongest outcome licensed without converting mechanism evidence into historical evidence.

This ordering preserves the main scientific distinction: SCH first asks what a single dual-audience trait does to fitness; only then does it ask whether the response is compromise, conditional/component separation, maintained polymorphism, or divergence. BITA's second trait and interaction estimand are not needed to make these one-trait outcome claims.
