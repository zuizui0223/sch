# SCH experimental pollination synthesis — real cross-study quantitative pilot v1

## Immediate result: the typical selection-attenuation hypothesis remains unsupported

The SCH flagship manuscript currently functions as a conceptual Viewpoint plus selected ecological evidence, not a newly collected field-study paper. This document instead reports a **new quantitative reanalysis of original published numeric treatment estimates**, performed with a complete within-source trait inventory and source-level replication. It is the first formal independent-programme meta pilot added to SCH; it is NOT a completed systematic literature meta-analysis or an ecological population prevalence estimate.

Source and executable receipt: \`data/SCH_META_EIGHT_PROGRAMME_EXPERIMENTAL_POLLEN_READOUT_V1.json\`, generated by \`scripts/analyze_sch_experimental_pollen_meta_8_programmes.py\`, and reproducible through \`.github/workflows/sch_experimental_pollen_meta_8.yml\`.

## Meta question 1: do pollination interventions commonly weaken realized selection?

For each source-matched floral trait in a fixed contextual background, calculate \`D_abs = |beta_open| - |beta_hand|\` on its ORIGINAL reported selection-model scale. Positive values indicate weaker absolute selection under pollen supplementation; negative values indicate stronger. Compute the mean of all original eligible axes *within each independent programme*. Do not treat the total 41 edges or sub-2021 sites as independent meta replications.

**Actual result:** **6/8 programme-average differences are positive**; exact two-sided programme-cluster sign P = **0.2890625**. The point proportion is not a natural ecological probability. Excluding the original Trifolium source lacking arm-specific SE yields **6/7**, exact two-sided P = **0.125**.

These tests do not support a general tendency for pollen supplementation to attenuate natural selection. The original study-specific effects are heterogeneous even in direction: for example the NEW Brown & Caruso *Lobelia siphilitica* data have an average stronger source-gradient magnitude under supplementation (mean D_abs = −0.0295 for seed production), with a similar point direction for fruit production (−0.0275) and reduced-pollen vs supplemented seed analysis (−0.03467). This is one programme-level contextual observation, not source-confirmed slope-equivalence or an evolutionary response.

The original paper Santangelo, Thompson & Johnson (2019; DOI 10.1111/jeb.13392) already reports pollinator treatment effects conditional on cyanogenic plant defence; the main table lacks cell SEs so our uncertainty-frozen source remains one sign-only contrast. The DOI itself was separately verified via original SCH document \`docs/SCH_H2_TRIFOLIUM_RECOVERY_V1.md\` and primary publisher reference; do not invent beta SE values.

## Meta question 2: how much of an apparent selection-direction reversal is actually supported?

In **16 of 41** source-matched comparisons, the fitted signs of beta_open and beta_hand differ. This figure is only an observed *point-estimate* count, not an incidence rate of ecological sign reversal. In only **one of the 40 pairs with cell SE**, the opposite directions are each individually nominally supported at two-sided 5%: *Gymnadenia* flowering onset under herbivores excluded. It is not a simultaneously familywise-supported reversal.

With unknown covariance in the original fitted within-study beta contrasts, use the always-valid covariance-compatible bound \`SE(beta_open - beta_hand) <= SE_open+SE_hand\` (conditional on validity of original source marginal SEs). An exploratory normal-Wald Bonferroni across **all 40 SE-equipped pairs** leaves **zero** source-reconstructed change certificates. This is not a declaration that source paper fitted interaction tests had no significant effects. For example Aconitum original nectar placement reports its own trait×pollen treatment test P=.015 (one-source study comparison), but this is not a 40-comparison familywise certificate. The source-level distinction matters.

## Meta question 3: is there a cross-species quantitative direction on an approximately common flower-number trait?

A separate and explicitly restricted **five-study sensitivity** compares standardized total-selection beta for *number of flowers/flower production per plant*, with relative female reproductive fitness as source endpoint:

| Study | beta_open − beta_supplemented | Max source-covariance-compatible SE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Dactylorhiza | +0.300 | 0.136 |
| Primula | +0.050 | 0.068 |
| Gymnadenia | +0.070 (average of 2 herbivory backgrounds in same programme) | 0.097 |
| Lobelia cardinalis | +0.240 | 0.290 |
| Aconitum | −0.023 | 0.080 |

Assuming independence **between those five original programmes** and normal asymptotics, but no independence between source treatment-cell estimates, inverse-upper-variance weighting gives:

    weighted conditional mean beta difference     +0.06192294
    covariance-compatible SE upper bound           0.04284510
    nominal normal 95% interval                    [−0.0220519, +0.1458978]
    two-sided normal P upper                        0.148381

**The interval crosses zero.** The test does not establish a cross-species mean intervention effect, and published trait standardizations and model covariates are not perfectly identical. This is a conservative, finite-source **fixed-effect sensitivity**, *not* a random-effects taxon-level meta-analysis, an estimated ecological population mean or a general statement that pollinator effects are zero*. Heterogeneity estimation with only five convenience-selected source studies would not produce a reliable population law.

## Why the original 2018 large meta-analysis source is not treated as recovered

Caruso, Eisen, Martin & Sletvold's larger published floral-selection meta-analysis assembled a much larger experimental literature, with a Dryad source DOI **10.5061/dryad.2v8c5g0**. The original unduplicated workbook \`Exp_stud_NOTdup_Dryad.xls\` and a deliberately duplicated downstream workbook \`Exp_stud_dup_Dryad.xlsx\` are listed in Dryad metadata. However, the genuine original file bytes could NOT be retrieved in this execution:

- public Dryad metadata endpoint: available
- Dryad \`/api/v2/files/{file_id}/download\`: HTTP **401 Unauthorized**
- public \`/downloads/file_stream/{file_id}\`: HTTP **403 Forbidden**
- public \`/stash/downloads/file_stream/{file_id}\`: HTTP **403 Forbidden**

These were checked in separate actual GitHub Actions runs with the source-digest validator \`scripts/ingest_sch_caruso2018_meta_source.py\`. The workflow is now **manual-only** rather than a repeatedly failing background CI. No original 2018 source coefficients, duplicate rows or unverifiable covariance values were silently copied. Therefore these eight studies remain a limited, partly newer source-compiled pilot and cannot claim to supersede the established 2018 meta-analysis.

The pooled denominator is a purposively assembled fixed experimental study roster, with source conditioning and nonconstant fitness and link scales. Importantly, the original Caruso paper (2018/2019) analyzed differences in **strength** of pollinator-mediated selection, so our separate questions about two-arm sign reversal and directly supported directional shift require their own registration and uncertainty control; this pilot cannot be presented as a replication of or challenge to that paper's full universe.

## Result for paper positioning

This empirical numerical pilot does not support a universal "pollinators generally change or reverse selection" conclusion, and it does not identify SCH's causal pure-function optima or loss L. The nontrivial methodological result is that **sign crossing is abundant as a source point-estimate observation but very rarely source-certifiable as two opposing directions**, while the narrowly comparable cross-study flower-count slope mean also remains uncertain.

That is an original, documented cross-study calculation, but **not yet a major positive macroecology paper**. A publishable standalone ecological macro-analysis would require either a sufficiently complete, source-identifiable external database with covariance support and a sign-blind eligible study universe, or replicated *original organism-level* data with comparable stage and trait definitions. Preserve the Viewpoint as a conceptual paper; use this pilot to prioritize a genuinely independent quantitative question instead of describing the same literature summaries as fresh results.

### Governing scope

- Primary independent unit: original experimental biological programme, n=8.
- Primary outcome: within-programme average absolute standardized selection-gradient change.
- No source-independent *between-species taxon* sample probability: avoid frequency estimates.
- No source full study-level covariance: use source-SE compatible bounds for a small, defined continuous trait.
- Original studies chosen for existing SCH/source recovery, not by a registered comprehensive PRISMA gate.
- Source values are published numeric source tables, **not** independent original field plant rows (contrast with SCH's separately completed Ipomopsis original-data result). This is not a new field experiment and not an analysis of independent original field plant rows.
- Original real palaeoclimate/phylogeny/biogeography moderators absent; this is a selected-study meta pilot, NOT geographic macroecology.
