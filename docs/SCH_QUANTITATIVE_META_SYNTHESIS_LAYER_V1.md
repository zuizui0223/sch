# SCH quantitative meta-synthesis layer v1

## Role

SCH now uses two empirical layers after the mathematical mechanism is established:

1. **strict architecture-pattern ledger** — source-adjudicated studies that bear on a shared coordinate, opposing functional demands, compromise, context shifts, and negative controls;
2. **external quantitative moderator synthesis** — existing meta-analyses that estimate how one interaction class changes floral traits, pollinator attraction, and plant reproductive output across many studies.

The two layers must not be pooled as if they estimate the same quantity.

## Current external quantitative anchors

### Moreira et al. 2019

`A meta-analysis of herbivore effects on plant attractiveness to pollinators` (Ecology; DOI 10.1002/ecy.2707).

Recovered aggregate pattern:

- overall herbivore effects were negative across floral traits, pollinator attractiveness, and reproductive success;
- effect strength/sign depended on tissue attacked and whether damage was real or simulated;
- the published synthesis reported substantial among-study heterogeneity.

Admitted SCH role:

```text
ANTAGONIST_WEIGHT_HAS_SYSTEMATIC_DOWNSTREAM_EFFECTS
MODERATOR_DEPENDENCE_IS_REAL
EFFECTS_PROPAGATE_THROUGH_FLORAL_TRAITS_TO_POLLINATOR_LANE_AND_FITNESS
```

Not admitted:

```text
SAME_COORDINATE_CONFLICT_IDENTIFIED
SEPARATED_FUNCTION_SPECIFIC_OPTIMA_IDENTIFIED
SCH_LOSS_L_DIRECTLY_ESTIMATED
```

### Updated synthesis, 2026

`The effect of herbivory on pollinators: a revisited meta-analysis` (Annals of Botany, 2026).

The updated synthesis reports:

```text
171 studies
1348 independent study cases
```

and again finds average reductions in floral traits, pollinator attraction and reproductive success, with strong moderation by damaged tissue and natural versus simulated damage.

This is useful because the central SCH mechanism predicts that the effective compromise depends on the relative weights of functions. The meta-analysis does not estimate an SCH optimum, but it independently establishes that antagonist pressure is not a fixed background term: its downstream effect on the pollinator-facing and reproductive lanes varies systematically with ecological context.

## Integration rule

Use the strict ledger to answer:

> Do real systems repeatedly show the observable signatures required by shared-coordinate conflict and compromise?

Use the quantitative external meta-layer to answer:

> Are the ecological weights entering those conflicts systematically variable across contexts, and can those changes propagate to floral phenotype, pollinator attraction and reproduction?

Do **not** treat the meta-analytic Hedges' g values as estimates of `L`, `z_P*`, `z_G*`, or `z_C*`.

## Next quantitative recovery

The next quantitative task is not to force all SCH studies into one grand effect. It is to recover compatible sub-lanes where the same estimand is available across independent studies, preferably:

1. standardized selection gradients on the same trait through two functional components;
2. experimental context contrasts that alter a selection gradient or nonlinear surface;
3. common-scale reproductive effects with reported uncertainty.

Until at least one such lane contains several independent biological clusters with compatible uncertainty, `quantitative_pool_eligible` remains false in the strict SCH pattern ledger.

## Claim ceiling

The combined empirical programme can presently claim recurrent conflict/context signatures plus independent quantitative evidence that antagonistic effects on floral-pollinator-reproductive pathways are moderator-dependent. It cannot claim that the external meta-analysis directly estimates the SCH mathematical compromise load or proves that every included study was generated by the SCH mechanism.
