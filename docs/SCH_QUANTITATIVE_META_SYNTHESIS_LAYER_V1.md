# SCH quantitative meta-synthesis layer v1

## Role

SCH uses two empirical layers after the mathematical conflict-identification mechanism is established:

1. **strict architecture-pattern ledger** — source-adjudicated systems bearing on one shared coordinate, opposing demands, compromise, context shifts, negative controls, and estimand boundaries;
2. **external quantitative moderator synthesis** — published meta-analyses estimating how ecological interaction classes alter floral traits, pollinator attraction, and reproductive output across many studies.

The two layers must not be pooled as if they estimate the same quantity.

## Current external quantitative anchors

### Moreira et al. 2019

`A meta-analysis of herbivore effects on plant attractiveness to pollinators` (Ecology; DOI 10.1002/ecy.2707).

Admitted SCH role:

```text
ANTAGONIST_WEIGHT_HAS_SYSTEMATIC_DOWNSTREAM_EFFECTS

EFFECTS_PROPAGATE_THROUGH_FLORAL_TRAITS_TO_POLLINATOR_LANE_AND_FITNESS
```

### Updated synthesis, 2026

The revisited herbivory–pollinator synthesis reports 171 studies and 1348 study cases and again supports average reductions in floral traits, pollinator attraction, and reproductive success with strong moderation by damage context. Its role is to show that ecological weighting is systematically variable, not to estimate SCH optima or conflict loss.

## Integration rule

Use the strict ledger to answer:

> Do independent biological systems repeatedly show literature-observable signatures compatible with shared-coordinate conflict, compromise, context weighting, or aligned/no-conflict alternatives?

Use the external quantitative meta-layer to answer:

> Are ecological weights entering those potential conflicts systematically variable across contexts, and can those changes propagate to floral phenotype, pollinator attraction, and reproduction?

Do **not** treat published Hedges' g values as estimates of `L`, `z_F1*`, `z_F2*`, state-specific reproductive optima, or direct causal compromise.

## Strict within-SCH quantitative recovery

The strict numerical programme targets compatible sub-lanes, preferably:

1. standardized component selection on the same trait coordinate;
2. directly estimated nonlinear component surfaces / optimum locations;
3. matched context contrasts that alter a gradient or surface;
4. common-scale reproductive effects with identified uncertainty.

Current strict conflict-design candidates are Dalechampia, Silene, Fragaria, and Gymnadenia. Exact numerical completeness and contrast uncertainty remain insufficient for a defensible random-effects pool under the frozen promotion rule.

A future positive-only pool must be labelled conditional if admission depends on observing the focal opposing-sign pattern. A design-wide average would require a sign-independent design-eligibility rule and retention of aligned/no-conflict systems in the same eligible universe.

## Claim ceiling

The combined empirical programme can claim recurrent conflict/context signatures plus independent quantitative evidence that antagonist effects on floral–pollinator–reproductive pathways are moderator-dependent. It cannot claim that the external meta-analysis directly estimates SCH mathematical loss, that a positive-selected pooled mean is an unbiased general effect, or that every coded study was generated by the SCH mechanism.
