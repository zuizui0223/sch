# Chapter 1 to Chapter 2 positioning

## Dissertation-level question

How do plants evolve floral signals when the same phenotype affects both mutualists and antagonists, and when can an additional defensive trait release that constraint?

## Chapter 1 — SCH: the one-trait conflict

SCH begins with one attraction/display coordinate `A`.

```text
W(A) = M(A) - G(A) - C(A)
```

Its empirical question is how pollinator benefit and antagonist loss move on that same coordinate. Its evolutionary question is whether the resulting surface supports:

- an integrated interior compromise;
- directional movement toward greater pollinator gain;
- directional movement toward lower antagonist exposure;
- disruptive branching on the same coordinate; or
- cue modularization that creates more separable coordinates.

The current positive result goes beyond existence of shared tracking. Case-level evidence recovers an integrated stabilizing compromise, context-dependent maintenance of alternative morphs, and population-level evolutionary change. Component partitioning and conditional emission recover partial decoupling mechanisms. The remaining historical gap is evolution from an ancestral shared cue to private modules or audience-specialized lineages.

## Chapter 2 — BITA: adding a defence coordinate

BITA asks whether adding a distinct antagonist-reducing trait `D` provides an escape route from the one-trait constraint. On a common outcome, the two-trait interaction is

```text
Delta_AD W = rho_delta - iota_delta - kappa_delta.
```

`D` is an effective escape route when antagonist relief at high attraction exceeds pollinator interference and the remaining joint cost. BITA's key contribution is to show that observing `Delta_AD W` does not identify that allocation, and to specify which crossed interventions and independent assay would do so.

## The two chapters answer one sequence

```text
Chapter 1: What evolutionary conflict exists on one shared signal coordinate?
        ↓
Chapter 2: Can a second defensive coordinate release that conflict, and can its mechanism be identified?
```

The chapters therefore share biological motivation but not estimands. SCH must not claim that BITA tested the one-trait hypothesis. BITA may use SCH to state the constraint that motivates `D`, then report positively how far current data recover relief, pollinator preservation, route separation, total interaction, and channel allocation.

## Joint claim ceiling

Together the current repositories support a staged result: dual-audience signal tracking is real; selective defence architectures recur; and current experiments usually stop before measuring the complete one-trait fitness geometry or the complete two-trait channel allocation. The missing intersections define specific experiments rather than an undefined call for more data.
