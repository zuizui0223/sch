## New source-level corrections in Batch 3

Batch 3 demonstrates why record-level P1 status cannot substitute for source-level trait-axis identification.

### Cypripedium candidum — reinforcement

Flowering stem height is a true same-axis case, but the two ecological routes reinforce rather than oppose one another.

Taller stems:

```text
pollination/fertilization success ↑
seed predation exposure           ↓
```

Both pathways therefore favor greater flowering height.

### Ursinia calenduliflora — conflict

The spotted floral morph receives a pollinator-facing benefit but a florivore-facing cost.

```text
spotted morph
  -> dominant pollinator preference / likely male-fitness benefit
  -> greater ovule predation by florivores
```

This is retained as a discrete same-signal conflict axis.

### Haleakalā silversword — one-sided geometry

Floral display size predicts pollinator visitation, whereas seed predation is associated with plant isolation rather than floral display.

Thus:

```text
display -> pollinator route
display -/-> detected seed-predator route
```

The result is one-sided rather than conflicted.

### Incarvillea sinensis — role-dependent boundary

The same bumblebee species can act as legitimate pollinator and nectar robber.

Flower height predicts robbery, but robbery does not reduce the measured seed outcomes and robbed flowers show more stigmatic closure.

A fixed mutualist-versus-antagonist sign is therefore inappropriate.

### Pulsatilla alpina — function-attribution downgrade

The record initially looked like a pollinator-versus-seed-predator height case.

Primary-source inspection shows that the fertilization component can contain both pollinator-mediated and selfing contributions. The study therefore identifies nonlinear fertilization and nonpredation geometry, but not a pure pollinator-specific component suitable for fixed-role H1.

The axis is retained but downgraded from the pollinator-versus-antagonist geometry stratum.

### Costa Rican disturbance mosaic — shared-axis downgrade

The study compares patch-level hummingbird visitation, fruit set and nectar piercing across disturbance types. These are ecological context contrasts, not two responses to one declared plant trait coordinate.

It therefore leaves H1 despite containing both mutualist and exploiter information.

### Andean Haplopappus odor gradient — shared-axis downgrade

Floral odor blends, visitor preference, fertile seeds and damaged seeds all vary with elevation.

However, seed-predator response to the same odor blend is not identified. Elevation covariation is not silently promoted to an odor-specific antagonist response.

## Consumer-role architecture

Nine axes now sit outside fixed-role H1 because consumer function is not constant.

```text
ROLE_DEPENDENT          4 axes
BENEFIT_COST_COUPLED    5 axes
```

Examples include:

- legitimate visitation versus robbing changing with floral access geometry;
- the same bumblebee acting as pollinator and nectar robber;
- pollen-feeding beetles damaging signal structures while pollinating;
- brood pollinators coupling pollination with later seed consumption.

This supports treating **consumer role** as an ecological state variable rather than assigning sign from taxonomic or behavioral labels alone.

## Source-level promotion and demotion are both results

The cumulative layer now records three explicit source-level downgrades.

This is important for SCH:

```text
record appears biologically relevant
!=
trait axis identifies functional geometry
```

A robust macro synthesis therefore needs both:

- promotion of valid same-axis cases;
- demotion of correlated-context, mixed-attribution, and non-shared-axis cases.

## Current ecological synthesis

The strongest bounded ecological statement is now:

> **Realized multifunctional geometry is assembled at trait axis × ecological context × consumer role. Even within pollinator-antagonist systems, the same broad interaction setting can generate conflict, reinforcement, one-sided effects, nonlinear cancellation, or role-coupled outcomes.**
