# Lythrum source-integrated ecological result: compensatory growth and selection are not the same response

## Biological question and exact source

Can an antagonist-associated perturbation **increase the mean display of a reproductive trait while weakening net selection for even more of that trait**, and can it intensify phenology selection despite a detectable pollen limitation that does not produce measurable pollinator-mediated selection on those traits?

**YES, as an original-study, source-bounded observation.** The 2017 field experiment of Thomsen & Sargent (*Annals of Botany*, DOI 10.1093/aob/mcx026) manipulated apical-meristem clipping (artificial, standardized herbivore-like damage) and used paired, within-plant flower supplementation. Our source audit joins the published Table 1 treatment-specific trait means with the *already frozen* Table 2 direct selection gradients and author ANCOVA-mediated contrasts. This is ONE independent published biological experiment already included among the SCH H2 local-selection programmes, **not** a new randomized source dataset, plant-level reconstructed table, fifth V27 pollen programme or extra natural population.

Primary full-text source: https://academic.oup.com/aob/article/119/8/1295/3084712 (2017), original Tables 1–2 and Methods.

### A phenotype versus selection paradox in authentic field measurements

| Trait | Control source mean (SE) | Clipped source mean (SE) | Observed phenotype response | Source direct selection β: control -> clipped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of inflorescences | 5.4 (0.72) | 8.0 (0.51) | **+48.15%** mean inflorescences | **+0.47 -> +0.20** (weaker further-increase selection) |
| Flowering start, Julian day | 191 (0.98) | 196 (1.06) | **+5 days**, later start | **-0.03 -> -0.28** (stronger earlier-flowering selection) |
| Number of flowers | 825 (80.6) | 1048 (83.4) | **+27.03%** mean flowers | No source selection gradient on this exact phenotype; do not infer one |
| Number of fruit | 445 (43.2) | 584 (46.9) | **+31.24%** mean fruits | Not a floral trait/selection-gradient contrast |
| Flowering end, Julian day | 236 (0.68) | 236 (0.75) | No difference of source group means | No source treatment-specific end-date selection slope |
| Seeds per fruit | 39.6 (3.02) | 43.7 (2.65) | Change not statistically detected in original Table 1 (P=0.3168) | Not a pollination-mediated-selection estimate |

The reported group-mean calendar interval between flowering onset and flowering end is thus **45 days** in control versus **40 days** in clipped plants, from subtraction of separately reported mean dates. This is an **illustrative group-mean calendar-window proxy**, NOT a measured distribution of individual flowering durations or a source-supported test of the change in per-plant flowering-period length.

The authors' original Table 1 tests support treatment-associated differences in inflorescence number (P<0.0001), flowering onset (P<0.0001), total flowers (P=0.0244) and fruit number (P=0.0275). Tests for inflorescence number and fruit number were performed after source-reported transformations, so these exact percentages describe SOURCE REPORTED MEANS, not coefficients or percentage-point selection estimates on the model scale.

### Published trait-selection shift and the three channels

Original Table 2 does not merely show two separately significant groups: it reports a direct **damage-treatment x trait ANCOVA selection contrast**, rather than requiring guessed covariance between treatment-arm slopes.

| Standardized focal trait | Source β control | Source β clipped | Direct ANCOVA Δβ control-minus-clipped, SE | Original source finding |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Inflorescence number | +0.47 | +0.20 | **+0.27 (0.10)** | P<0.01 |
| Inflorescence height | +0.33 | +0.22 | +0.11 (0.11) | Not significant |
| Flowering onset | -0.03 | -0.28 | **+0.25 (0.10)** | P<0.05 |

By contrast, the original **pollination treatment x trait** and **pollination x clipping x trait** terms were not reported significant for any of these three traits. At the same time, within-plant hand-supplemented flowers produced **17.4% more seeds** on average than naturally pollinated flowers, i.e. measurable pollen limitation. Degree of pollen limitation was not detectably different between clipped and control treatments (reported P=0.2718), **which is not an equivalence test**.

Therefore, actual pollen limitation does **not** automatically identify pollinator-mediated *directional selection on a particular floral trait*, and a disturbance-induced change in average trait expression is not interchangeable with the change in net selection gradient on that trait.

### Inferential sensitivity without inventing model covariances

The author directly reports SE on the three damage x trait interactions. A SECONDARY, non-preregistered normal approximation using these directly fitted SEs gives original-table-derived nominal P of approximately 0.00693 for inflorescence number and 0.0124 for flowering onset. Across the **three declared damage terms only**, both pass a three-term Bonferroni normal-Wald check at family alpha=.05; when original source beta and SE are each allowed half a last printed decimal of rounding uncertainty, only the inflorescence-number contrast remains certified.

Expanding the exploratory family to **all nine** author-supplied interactions (three traits x [damage, pollination, damage x pollination]) yields **no conservative 9-test Bonferroni certificate**. This is a calculation on printed source coefficients, NOT a refitted ANCOVA, a refutation of the original author-reported individual tests, evidence for null pollination impact or a familywise guarantee that the study itself preregistered.

### The ecological fork relevant to SCH's theory

The preceding SCH manuscript results highlighted two sources of trait-selection change:

1. *Gymnadenia conopsea*: genuine factorial pollination x herbivory experiment; flowering onset carries opposing source-point contributions, and a pollination manipulation changes net directional selection while the herbivory background is held fixed. Spur length in the same experimental programme can instead show reinforcing source-point contributions.
2. *Lythrum salicaria*: simulated apical meristem damage shifts floral architecture and phenology through **plausible compensatory regrowth**, with direct reported damage-associated selection contrasts. A detectable pollen seed-set deficit does not coincide with a detected pollination-mediated shift on the analysed architecture and flowering-time traits.

These are **biologically distinct paths for ecological state to change trait selection**. The first centers altered mutualist service and the balance of opposing influences; the second centers regrowth/reproductive allocation after vegetative damage. Neither alone establishes a general prevalence of each mechanism, universal adaptive response or historically evolved tolerance.

The especially interesting falsifiable prediction is that **ecological disturbance can produce phenotype–selection discordance: the disturbed state may be phenotypically richer in a reproductive structure while selection for additional investment in that structure weakens**. Relative fitness and within-treatment standardized predictors are the author's own definition; the observed paradox is not a contradiction of natural selection theory. Even greater branching can coexist with cost to further branching or selection for earlier recovery.

### Source design and causal ceiling

- May 2013: 178 stems initially selected, final analysed n=173 (clipped 87, control 86). Initial assignment was randomized, but **eight natural-attack control stems were reassigned to the clipped category** in the published design. Consequently the displayed treatment comparison is not a completely preserved original intention-to-treat contrast; causal conclusions must retain this protocol limitation.
- All stems were sleeved to control insect access; clipping removed approximately 2 cm of the apical meristem, simulating Neogalerucella feeding and browsing. It is not randomized natural herbivory, nor a predator treatment on reproductive structures.
- Pollination was randomized between flowers in selected within-plant matched pairs (429 pairs, not 429 independent plants). The investigators extrapolated plant seed production using total fruit counts and treatment-specific sampled flowers. This is not randomized whole-plant pollinator service in SCH's full z x P x G causal state design.
- Inflorescence traits and flowering time were OBSERVED after clipping, **not randomized independently**. Source Table 2 is selection conditional on damaged/undamaged states, not identification of the isolated developmental regrowth mediator. No source-matched raw plant covariances, genotype-by-environment effects, male function or complete field trait fitness optima were newly recovered.
- None of this identifies SCH pure-function optima, conflict budget L, BITA dimension allocation, or SLK architecture value.
