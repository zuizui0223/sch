# Brassica rapa 2017: aligned attraction, downstream reproductive antagonism, and trait-combination selection

## Scientific result and why it differs from another reversal-rate audit

The SCH question is often framed as "which partner selects for a greater or smaller floral trait?" Yet an interaction partner may **both pollinate and cause herbivory through its offspring**, so the sign of *attraction* cannot be equated with the sign of the eventual *reproductive fitness contribution*. The experiment of **Knauer & Schiestl (2017), Evolutionary Ecology 31:285–304; DOI 10.1007/s10682-016-9878-8**, supplies a rare same-study, multi-endpoint demonstration without pretending to be a causal randomized trait-optimum experiment.

The original experiment exposed *Brassica rapa* plants to **bumblebees only, cabbage butterflies only, or both** in controlled cages. *Pieris brassicae* **adults pollinated flowers**, while their **offspring fed on Brassica** following oviposition. Hence "cabbage butterfly" is not an antagonist-only category. In the source's own interpretation, the butterfly can be beneficial when no alternative pollinator is present yet impose a net herbivore cost in the presence of a more effective pollinator. It is incorrect to code "CABBAGE_BUTTERFLY" as a pure seed-predator G treatment or the mixed treatment as a full 2x2 selective pollinator/predator factorial.

### Result 1 — The same signals attract both flower visitors and butterfly oviposition

The source Table 1 reports three **distinct response models** for nine floral traits: bumblebee visits to inflorescences, cabbage-butterfly visits, and cabbage-butterfly **egg presence**. Both flower size and phenylacetaldehyde had **source-nominal positive associations in all three channels**:

| Floral trait | Bumblebee visiting β±SE (source P) | Butterfly visiting β±SE (source P) | Butterfly oviposition β±SE (source P) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corolla size | +0.24±0.05 (<0.001) | +0.15±0.04 (<0.001) | +0.45±0.16 (0.006) |
| Phenylacetaldehyde | +0.15±0.06 (0.010) | +0.09±0.04 (0.029) | +0.41±0.17 (0.016) |
| (E)-alpha-farnesene | +0.18±0.05 (<0.001) | −0.05±0.04 (0.19) | −0.16±0.16 (0.35) |

All NINE trait axes, including the six other source-defined controls, are frozen in the exact original 27-row Table 1 ledger. Thus the nominal *three-response overlap* is **2 of 9 studied floral traits**, not an invented denominator based on the two favorable rows selected after inspection. This is NOT a probability-sampled ecological prevalence fraction. It is a single original experimental system's source-level pattern, and source Table 1 P values are **not** familywise adjusted across 27 exploratory tests.

Crucially, Table 1 coefficients are fitted to **different outcome scales**: counts of inflorescence visits versus eggs present/absent. The beta values therefore **cannot be compared in magnitude**, summed into a consumer "preference weight", or converted into a cost of future seed loss. Insect composition was experimentally imposed; flower size and fragrance emission were measured covariates, **not randomized interventions**. Attraction and oviposition co-associations do not establish that one signal directly caused all three responses.

A third source trait, (E)-alpha-farnesene, was positively associated with **bee visitation** but its association with butterfly visits and oviposition was **not detected**. This is a plausible *receiver-biased candidate signal*, not proof that butterflies cannot respond to it: absence of detected butterfly effects is neither an equivalence test nor a confirmed private communication channel. The study provides neither a prospective butterfly-neutrality margin nor trait-by-receiver randomization for that promotion.

### Result 2 — Net selection need not oppose the signal even when oviposition follows it

The existing frozen Table 2 provides NINE flower trait selection gradients under each of the THREE insect-composition regimes, with **relative seed set** as the reproductive endpoint. Across all regimes, **corolla size had positive selection**: bee only **+0.22±0.04 (P<0.001)**, mixed **+0.22±0.08 (P=0.005)**, and butterfly only **+0.26±0.09 (P=0.007)**.

By contrast, phenylacetaldehyde selection was **positive and individually significant only in the bumblebee-only regime** (+0.15±0.05, P=0.006); the mixed-regime source beta was **−0.09±0.08, P=0.27** and the butterfly-only beta **+0.08±0.10, P=0.18**. This is a source difference in *which cell estimates reach significance*, **not** a demonstrated statistical reversal or equivalence of selection across regimes. Importantly, the paper **did not detect a trait x insect-treatment directional-selection interaction by its overall ANCOVA**. The apparent conditional scent pattern must not be narrated as a source-confirmed slope difference based solely on separate P thresholds.

This gives a concrete **stage-translation** counterexample: positive oviposition association with a floral trait can coexist with **positive net seed-fitness selection on that trait** in the same studied regime. The plant may still benefit from corolla enlargement in observed environments, despite the egg-laying cost. Neither net positive selection nor mutualist/antagonist attractiveness overlap alone reveals a hidden, negatively selected *pure* antagonist trait optimum.

### Result 3 — Reproductive losses and two-trait coupling occur downstream

The same original experiment reported positive fitted seed-production associations for **bumblebee flower visits** (+20.5±4.8 source β±SE) and **butterfly flower visits** (+6.8±2.0), but a **negative caterpillar herbivory association** (−104.5±19.0); the bee visitation × infestation fitness interaction was also **negative** (−18.9±9.5, source P=0.046). This directly establishes that the butterfly's adult visitor and offspring herbivore routes carry different fitness contributions; the Table 1 visitation betas are **not on these seed-fitness scales**. These original regression effects were not recovered as a new causal mediation model.

Six flower-trait interaction pairs across three regimes were evaluated in the original Table 3: **18 correlational-selection tests**. In the **mixed bee–butterfly regime only**, the corolla-size × phenylacetaldehyde selection interaction was nominally negative **γ=−0.17±0.08, P=0.029**. It is the sole source Table 3 test with P<0.05 but **fails** the complete 18-test 5%-family Bonferroni cutoff (0.05/18≈0.00278). The original authors also reported **no significant among-regime ANCOVA effect on correlational selection**. Therefore a strongly established across-regime change in multi-trait geometry cannot be claimed.

**The original paper independently reported nonadditive selection under a specific biological additive benchmark:** the mixed-insect linear trait gradient should equal the arithmetic average of the two single-insect gradients, because relative fitness and half-as-intense pure-species exposure were used. Source-fitted departures included phenylacetaldehyde **−0.20±0.08, P=0.009** and 1-butene-4-isothiocyanate **+0.19±0.08, P=0.018**. A simple arithmetic check on printed Table 2 yields for phenylacetaldehyde expected (+0.15+0.08)/2=+0.115 versus actual mixed −0.09, deviation −0.205 (consistent with −0.20 after table rounding); for the isothiocyanate expected (−0.11−0.13)/2=−0.12 versus mixed +0.07, deviation +0.19. These are source **author-fitted** nonadditivity tests under a conditional reference model, not our own guessed covariance refits, and have not been declared significant after applying a study-wide multiplicity correction.

### Result 4 — The mechanistic generalization is stage-dependent role sign, not a static "conflict" category

This experiment adds **one source-backed ecological reality anchor** to the already coded SCH H2 *Brassica* biological programme; it is **not** a new independent case in the SCH 16-cluster concept ledger or another independent replication of the Gymnadenia/Lythrum experiments. It illuminates two SCH predictions:

- The *same signal* can convey a **positive attraction cue** to a useful visitor and to egg-laying adults whose offspring impose a **negative downstream seed-fitness pathway**. The sign of receiver preference does not directly determine the sign of net natural selection.
- A conflict-compatible ecological system can show **positive univariate selection on one shared signal** while the hypothesized reproductive cost may appear in *fitness interaction* or *trait-combination terms* rather than as a source-supported negative univariate gradient. These dimensions must be distinguished and their uncertainties retained.

The source-aligned trajectory from receiver attraction → oviposition → larval herbivory → final seed fitness is biological evidence for potential **role-dependent fitness translation**, but individual flowers/seed fates and causal mediation were not separately reconstructed in these Table 1–3 aggregates. Nor was corolla or volatile emission randomized. A truly selective BITA-like differentiated signaling axis would require showing that a substitute signal preserves pollination while avoiding antagonistic recruitment at a **predefined equivalence margin**, with a measured dimensional cost. The (E)-alpha-farnesene response is an interesting candidate, **not proof of such a solution**.

This is why *Pedicularis rex* exsertion and *Brassica rapa* signaling are complementary biological systems. P. rex has observational pollen/predation associations and a bract-defence perturbation from separate original source studies; Brassica provides multi-receiver visitation, oviposition and relative-fitness signatures **inside one controlled consumer-composition experiment**, but still does not manipulate the trait coordinate. Neither source supplies the complete crossed z × P × G causal fitness surfaces or the numerical shared conflict budget L required to certify the SCH→SLK architecture inference.
